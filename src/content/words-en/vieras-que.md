### Definition

«Vieras que» is a conversational opener common in Guatemala and Central America. It is used to get the listener's attention before telling a story or sharing news, functioning as a friendly discourse marker similar to "you see," "so you know," or "you won't believe it but."

Though it literally comes from the Spanish subjunctive of the verb «ver» (to see), its use has become lexicalized as a conversational device that prepares the listener for an anecdote, piece of news, or relevant information. It's a natural and polite way to introduce a new topic in informal conversation.

### Usage

In Guatemala, «vieras que» is used daily in casual conversations among friends, family, and acquaintances. It works as a gentle attention-getter that signals what you're about to say is interesting, surprising, or noteworthy. Unlike more direct interjections, «vieras que» has a warm and almost intimate tone.

For example, if someone wants to share exciting news, they might start with: «Vieras que I won a raffle at work». It's also used to excuse or explain a situation: «Vieras que I couldn't arrive early because traffic was terrible». The phrase eases the listener into what comes next.

- «Vieras que the corner store closed down.»
- «Vieras que I ran into your mom at the supermarket and she greeted me happily.»
- «Vieras que the dog ran away last night, but he came back on his own.»
